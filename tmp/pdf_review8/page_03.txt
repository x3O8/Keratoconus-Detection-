2 Related Work
Computational approaches to keratoconus detection fall broadly into four
groups: (i) traditional ML on structured tabular data, (ii) CNN-based deep
learning on corneal images, (iii) attention and transformer-based frameworks,
and (iv) knowledge-driven XAI systems. Broader reviews of AI in healthcare
consistently flag reliability, bias mitigation, and real-world deployability as
open challenges that any clinical system, including ours, must address [16].
Similar fusion-of-evidence, explain-the-decision approaches have been applied
elsewhere in medical AI – graph-theoretic characterisation of brain tumours
from MRI [4,19], deep learning for digitising handwritten prescriptions [17], ex-
plainability comparisons across X-ray and CT [30], contrast-free CBV perfusion
mapping for stroke assessment [25], and IoT-integrated ensemble classifiers for
remote monitoring [3] – suggesting this philosophy is converging independently
across several branches of the field. For readability, the three datasets used
for training and evaluation – a multi-class corneal topography image set (DS-
1), a structured biomechanical and clinical feature set (DS-2), and a binary
TFRecord-encoded image set (DS-3), each described in full in Section 5.2 – are
referred to throughout this paper by these shorthand labels.
2.1 Conventional and Traditional ML Diagnostics
Placido-disc topography, Scheimpflug tomography, and OCT perform well once
KC has reached a moderate stage but show limited sensitivity for FFKC and
SKC[8,31].ClassicalMLmodelstrainedontopographicindices–SVMs,decision
trees,andlogisticregression–generalisedpoorlyacrossscannersandpopulations.
Ensemble methods fared better: Muhsin et al. [15] report over 98% accuracy
using Random Forest and Gradient Boosting for KC severity staging, albeit on
a different cohort and label set than ours, and Uke et al. [29] targeted resource-
constrained deployment with a lightweight pipeline. All of these models remain
restricted to structured tabular data and cannot process raw corneal images.
2.2 Deep Learning Approaches
CNNs enabled automated hierarchical feature extraction directly from pixel-
level topography data. Yadav and Kaur [33] demonstrated EfficientNetB7 on
corneal tomograms; Alqazaz et al. [2] proposed a lightweight CNN for edge de-
vices; Sandhiya et al. [22] presented a custom CNN multi-class grader achieving
75.33% accuracy; and Ruiz et al. [21] showed that multimodal CNN fusion of
OCT and Scheimpflug imagery improves sensitivity over either modality alone.
