2.3 Transformer, Multimodal, and Knowledge-Driven Frameworks
Attention-based models overcome CNN locality bias by selectively weighting
relevant spatial regions [34], and Singh et al. [24] applied a vision-transformer
backbone directly to early KC detection. Wang et al. [32] identify cross-modal
attention and late fusion as the two dominant multimodal strategies in medi-
cal imaging more broadly. Keshavarz et al. [10] integrated structured medical
insights with imaging features to improve sensitivity. On the knowledge-driven
side, Chandak et al. [5] and Reddy and Veena [20] show that knowledge graphs
and graph-augmented language models can ground biomedical predictions in
curated clinical evidence, and RAG systems extend this idea to open-ended gen-
eration: Patel et al. [18] evaluated RAG for clinical decision support, directly
informing the reasoning module used here.
2.4 Research Gap
Prior work does not jointly address: (i) unified multimodal integration across
corneal topography images, spatial map grids, and biomechanical indices with at-
tention fusion; (ii) multi-head prediction providing simultaneous KC staging and
severity/progression-risk estimation; (iii) explainability with evidence-grounded
reasoning rather than saliency maps alone; and (iv) a scalable, clinically deploy-
able pipeline combining all of the above with LLM-based explanation. Table 1
summarises this gap against representative prior systems.
