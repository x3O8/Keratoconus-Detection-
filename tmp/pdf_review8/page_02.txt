Diagnosis today relies primarily on Placido-disc topography, Scheimpflug to-
mography, and anterior-segment optical coherence tomography (OCT). These
modalities are reliable once the cone is topographically apparent, but are demon-
strably less sensitive to Forme Fruste Keratoconus (FFKC) and Subclinical Ker-
atoconus (SKC) [31] – the stages at which biomechanical softening of the stroma
routinely precedes any topographically visible change, and where expert graders
have been shown to miss a meaningful fraction of subclinical cases [8,34].
Early detection matters because it is the only point at which intervention
can still change the disease trajectory. Corneal collagen cross-linking is currently
the only treatment with proven efficacy in halting KC progression, and its ef-
fectiveness declines once the cornea has already thinned and steepened substan-
tially [8,12]. A screening tool that flags FFKC and SKC with high sensitivity,
while also explaining why, therefore carries direct clinical value: it can turn a
borderline case that a clinician might otherwise monitor and re-scan months
later into an actionable referral today.

tational approaches to KC detection, however, remain fragmented in three ways:
image-only networks discard the structured biomechanical indices that clinicians
already trust [7,22]; classical ensemble methods trained purely on tabular data
cannot see topographic morphology at all [15,29]; and saliency-map explain-
able AI (XAI) methods, while visually compelling, provide neither statistical
coverage guarantees nor grounding in external clinical knowledge [13]. To our
knowledge, no prior work combines multimodal fusion, multi-task clinical pro-
filing, and knowledge-grounded reasoning within a single, clinically deployable
pipeline.
Problem Statement.Given multimodal corneal imaging data (topography im-
ages and spatial map grids) alongside structured biomechanical indices, this work
designs and implements a deep learning framework capable of detecting early-
stage and subclinical keratoconus with high sensitivity and specificity, while
producing clinically interpretable, evidence-grounded explanations suitable for
integration into ophthalmic screening workflows.
Contributions.This work makes four contributions:
1.Unified Multimodal Architecture.EfficientNet-B0 with spatial atten-
tion fusion processes corneal topography images and spatial map grids, while
XGBoost handles structured biomechanical indices; a learned ensemble com-
bines both modality streams into a single prediction.
2.Multi-Head Prediction.A shared backbone simultaneously outputs KC
stageclassification,acontinuousseverityandprogression-riskregression,and
inter-ocular asymmetry via binary cross-entropy, enabling clinical profiling
beyond static-stage grading alone.
