3.2 Multi-Head Prediction Architecture
Rather than framing prediction as a single classification question, the predic-
tion layer is treated as a multi-task learning problem, with a shared backbone
trained to answer three clinically distinct questions at once.Head 1 (stage)is a
three-way classifier separating Normal, Suspect (early signs, not yet diagnostic),
and Keratoconus (confirmed).Head 2 (severity/progression-risk)is a re-
gression head that outputs a continuous severity and progression-risk score from
the fused, single-visit representation, reported by the Output Layer (Section 4.4)
and used to flag patients whose current presentation warrants closer, more ur-
gent follow-up. It is architecturally designed to extend into a temporal encoder
over sequential scans of the same eye once longitudinal, multi-visit data be-
comes available (Section??), so the same head can evolve from a cross-sectional
severity estimate into a genuine rate-of-change predictor without redesigning
the backbone.Head 3 (asymmetry)is a binary cross-entropy head that de-
