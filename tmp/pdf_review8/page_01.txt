Abstract.Keratoconus (KC) is a progressive corneal ectatic disorder
characterised by corneal thinning and irregular steepening, which can
lead to visual impairment. Early detection remains challenging because
subtle disease may not be readily identified from a single source of clini-
cal evidence. This paper presents KeratoScan, an explainable research
decision-support workflow for binary keratoconus screening that inte-
grates corneal-map imaging and structured clinical measurements.
The image branch processes seven corneal-map inputs using a shared
EfficientNet-B0 backbone followed by four-head modality attention,
while the clinical branch uses a calibrated XGBoost model operating on
structured clinical variables. The branches can operate independently or,
when both inputs are available, be combined through user-configurable
late probability fusion. The system provides Grad-CAM and Integrated
Gradients image attributions, modality-attention rollout, and TreeSHAP
clinical-feature contributions to support inspection of model behaviour.
A retrieval component returns relevant passages from a curated kerato-
conus knowledge base; optional citation-constrained Gemini responses
are available when configured.
On the held-out binary DS-1 image test set (n= 150), the seven-map
EfficientNet-B0 model achieved 94.00% accuracy, 0.9904 AUROC, and
0.9290 macro-F1. On the locked clinical test set, XGBoost achieved
98.19% accuracy, 95.10% sensitivity, 97.49% F1 score, and 0.9994 AU-
ROC. The FastAPI and React dashboard displays branch-specific re-
sults, weighted fusion, uncertainty checks, model explanations, and re-
trieved clinical context. The combined late-fusion output has not yet
been validated on a matched patient-eye multimodal cohort; accordingly,
KeratoScan is presented as a research decision-support workflow rather
than a clinically validated diagnostic, staging, progression-prediction, or
treatment-recommendation system.
