However, the combined late-fusion output has not yet been validated on a
matched patient-eye cohort containing both image and clinical inputs. It should
therefore be interpreted as a research workflow for integrating independently
validated branch predictions, rather than as a separately validated multimodal
clinical model. The system performs binary screening only: it does not provide
validateddiseasestaging,severityestimation,progressionprediction,inter-ocular
asymmetry detection, or treatment recommendations. Likewise, Grad-CAM, In-
tegrated Gradients, attention weights, and SHAP values describe model be-
haviour rather than biological causation.
The current FastAPI and React dashboard is intended for research and
decision-support use only. It provides branch-specific predictions, weighted fu-
sion, explanation panels, uncertainty checks, and retrieval-based clinical context.
The evidence-retrieval component uses a curated knowledge base with FAISS-
based semantic retrieval and lexical fallback. Gemini-based, citation-constrained
responses are optional when configured; otherwise, a deterministic grounded re-
sponse is returned. Neither retrieved evidence nor generated text constitutes
patient-specific medical advice.
Future work should first establish a matched, prospective, multi-centre, longi-
tudinal patient-eye cohort to validate the late-fusion strategy, assess calibration
and uncertainty under scanner and population shift, and evaluate clinical utility
against clinician decision-making. Additional priorities include pre-specified ab-
lation studies of individual corneal maps and the multi-head modality-attention
module; external validation across institutions and devices; systematic assess-
ment of explanation stability; and expansion to clinically justified longitudinal
outcomes only after repeat-visit data are available. Before any clinical deploy-
ment, the system would also require robust privacy, security, usability, regulatory,
and prospective clinical validation studies.
